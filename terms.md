## 6. How moderation works, in plain language

**We don't review what gets published before it is published.** There is no person and no system
watching the whole catalogue: it would be impossible and the law doesn't require it. What there is, is a
system that reacts to reports.

**Anyone can report**, with or without an account. From the app, on a pack, on a sticker or on a
person's profile. Without the app, through the route published on this same site. You pick a reason and
can add a short explanation.

**Not all reasons weigh the same.** There are three levels, and the level decides what happens and how
fast:

| Level | Which reasons | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary | Spam or deception, wrong category, use of copyrighted material | Once several reports pile up, the entry is flagged for review. **It is not hidden** |
| Serious | Sexual content, graphic violence, hate speech, impersonation | With few reports, the entry **is hidden from the catalogue** until reviewed |
| Severe | Threat to life or safety, child abuse material | **A single report hides the entry immediately** |

We don't publish the exact numbers, and the reason is simple: publishing them would be explaining how
much it costs to take down someone else's work.

**Automatic hiding is not a verdict.** It is a precaution while someone looks: it may well end with the
entry going back to the catalogue exactly as it was. The review is done by a person.

**Deadlines.** The severe level is reviewed within hours. Everything else, within two weeks at most. That
is a long deadline and we say so with its reason: with automatic hiding nobody is left exposed
meanwhile, and the only thing that lasts is the invisibility of something that may have been innocent.

**The author is told.** If something of yours is hidden, you see it in "My packs", with the explanation
when there is one and with an "under review" when there isn't one yet because nobody has looked.

**Reporting is not blocking.** Blocking someone means you stop seeing them; we don't tell them and it
takes nothing away from them. It can be undone from Settings.

**Reporting in bad faith has consequences.** Repeatedly using reports to take down other people's work
is grounds for suspension, just like publishing what section 5 forbids.

**You can appeal a decision** by writing to `stickerfit.contact@gmail.com` with the link to what was removed. A person
replies, not an automated form.

**For manifestly illegal content we preserve the material and notify the competent authorities where
the law requires it.** No automation does this: a person decides.
